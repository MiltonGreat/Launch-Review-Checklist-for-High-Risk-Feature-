# Launch Review Checklist for High-Risk Platform Feature

**A mock regulatory launch review for a high-risk platform feature, demonstrating approval workflow design, cross-functional coordination, and status reporting.**

---

## What This Is

This repository contains a complete mock launch review package for a proposed platform feature ("Discovery Feed v2") that introduces personalised recommendations, sponsored posts, and premium subscription promotional cards to UK and EU users.

It demonstrates how a Regulations Analyst would:

1. **Define the proposed launch** and identify the regulatory requirements triggered by the change.
2. **Build a launch review checklist** mapping each requirement to a review question, a responsible team, and a status.
3. **Design an approval workflow** that requires sign-off from Legal, Product, Engineering, Compliance, and Regulations before production release.
4. **Produce a one-page status update memo** summarising what has changed, what is required, and what has been delivered.

The goal is not to provide legal interpretation. It is to demonstrate a **repeatable methodology** for regulatory launch review and approval coordination.

## Methodology

The launch review follows a four-step process:

1. **Define** — Describe the proposed product changes and the jurisdictions affected.
2. **Map** — Identify the regulatory requirements triggered by the change (EU DSA, UK Online Safety Act, UK consumer protection legislation).
3. **Check** — Build a checklist that asks a specific review question for each requirement, assigns a responsible team, and tracks status.
4. **Coordinate** — Produce an approval record and a status update memo that make the outstanding work visible to stakeholders.

This methodology is transferable to any high-risk product launch in a regulated industry.

## Regulatory Sources Used

The checklist is grounded in primary regulatory sources, not summaries:

- **EU Digital Services Act (DSA)** — Articles 26, 27, and 28 (advertising transparency, recommender transparency, protection of minors).
- **UK Online Safety Act (OSA)** — Sections 9–10 (illegal content risk assessment), 11–12 (children's risk assessment), 20–21 (reporting and complaints), and 23 (record-keeping).
- **UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers (DMCC) Act 2024** — Sections on unfair commercial practices, paid promotions, and subscription contracts.
- **Ofcom regulatory documents and Codes of Practice** — For tracking updated guidance and service-specific obligations.
- **UK Government subscription contracts guidance** — For monitoring forthcoming subscription-specific requirements (commencement date: 1 January 2027).

## Important Caveats

- **This is a demonstration project.** ConnectSphere is fictional. The requirements listed are proposed operational mappings, not legal determinations that every provision applies.
- **Legal interpretation is required.** Whether the feature constitutes a significant change requiring further risk assessments must be determined through the applicable legal and risk-assessment process.
- **The Online Safety Act does not impose a general equivalent of DSA Article 27.** Its relevance here is the assessment and mitigation of safety risks arising from algorithms and service design.
- **Not legal advice.** This project is for portfolio demonstration only.
